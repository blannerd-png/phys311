I learned that just because a graph looks like what you would expect physically, it can't always be trusted. A great example is that the graphs
from my last lab looked good, but I had made small errors in the order of my position and velocity updates. Another example is how the graphs from
mechanical energy looked as if it was a straight horizontal line because of the y-scale. When plotting the actual energy drift however, you can see
that the mechanical energy does not stay constant and the numerical approximations caused extra energy to either be added or removed from the system
depending on what method you were using. Another example is the expectation that the euler cromer method would create a graph where the error
oscillates since that is what you expect from that method, but since the force does rely on position in uniform gravity, that was not the case.
