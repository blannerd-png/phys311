I chose this project because I wanted to combine my interests in nuclear engineering, medical physics, and computational modeling. I'm particularly interested in how radiation interacts with biological materials and how those interactions can be used in medical imaging. A PET simulation allows me to apply concepts I've learned in my physics and nuclear engineering courses while also developing my programming skills. I also wanted to challenge myself by creating something that has a real-world application rather than just demonstrating a basic physics concept.

What scares me most about this project is the complexity of the physics involved and making sure my simulation is actually accurate. There are a lot of different processes to account for, including positron transport, annihilation, photon attenuation, and detection. I'm worried about finding the right balance between making realistic physical assumptions and keeping the simulation manageable within the time available. I'm also concerned about debugging the code and validating my results, since a simulation can run successfully without necessarily producing physically correct results. However, I think working through these challenges will help me become a better programmer and develop a stronger understanding of computational physics.
