This week, I made significant progress on my PET scanner simulation and completed the majority of the MUST-tier requirements for my final project. My simulation now includes relativistic positron-electron annihilation, photon transport, detector coincidences, and PET image reconstruction. I also implemented a fixed-timestep accumulator to separate the physics calculations from the rendering frame rate. Additionally, I added a debug HUD that displays energy conservation, momentum, FPS, and other diagnostic information, along with a conservative test mode that allows me to isolate and test the physics under ideal conditions.

One of my biggest accomplishments was running numerical validation tests to verify that the simulation behaves as expected. My energy and momentum conservation tests passed with extremely small numerical errors, and my photon attenuation results closely matched the theoretical Beer–Lambert prediction. I also performed CPU-throttling tests at different slowdown levels. Although the physics calculations remained consistent, I noticed that the simulation's frame rate decreased significantly under computational load, and the accumulator sometimes discarded elapsed time to maintain responsiveness. This is something I would like to improve.

Moving forward, I want to focus on optimizing performance, particularly for devices with less computational power. I also want to continue exploring how time-of-flight resolution, detector geometry, and Compton scattering affect image reconstruction and tumor detectability. Since my project is intended to help users visualize the physics behind PET imaging, I would also like to make sure that the simulation is intuitive and that the different settings and physical processes are clearly explained.

Overall, I feel that my project is close to being ready for final submission, especially because the core simulation is functional, deployed on GitHub Pages, and has passed its numerical validation tests. However, I would still like to spend some time improving performance, completing final browser checks, and polishing the documentation. I also think I want to make it more user friendly especially after recieving the midweek check in. I am happy with the progress I have made, and I think the project successfully demonstrates how the underlying physics of positron emission tomography contributes to the formation and quality of reconstructed images.
